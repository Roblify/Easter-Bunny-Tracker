🗺️ Using the Tracker
The tracker activates automatically when the Easter Bunny begins his journey on Easter morning. Keep an eye on the status display - it will update from "Preparing for takeoff…" to live tracking as soon as he's in the air.
"Last spotted" shows the most recent location the Easter Bunny departed from. As he moves from city to city, this field updates to reflect wherever he was just delivering eggs.
Every time the Easter Bunny finishes delivering at a location and moves on, a basket icon is left behind on the map to mark where he's already been. Click any basket marker to see information about that city!
If the Easter Bunny is already very close to your location - or has already passed through - the estimate will show "anytime" to let you know he's in your area. That means it's time to get to sleep!
Yes! Click the "🔓 Unlock Camera" button in the bottom-right corner of the tracker to freely pan, zoom, and explore the map. Press "🔒 Lock to Bunny" at any time to snap back to following the Easter Bunny automatically.
The City Info panel displays details about the Easter Bunny's current or next destination, including the local time there, population, elevation, and a link to its Wikipedia article. You can collapse it using the small arrow button in its corner if you'd prefer a cleaner view.
⚙️ Settings & Features
Streamer Mode hides the "Easter Bunny will arrive at your location in about…" estimate from the HUD. This prevents viewers of your broadcast from guessing your approximate location based on that arrival time. All other tracker information remains visible.
Cinematic Camera mode tilts and angles the camera to follow the Easter Bunny from the side rather than directly overhead, giving the tracker a more dynamic, movie-like feel. You can adjust the side offset angle (0° = behind, 90° = side, 180° = front) and the zoom level using the sliders that appear when it's enabled.
2D mode shows a traditional flat Mercator map projection. 3D mode switches to a globe view with atmospheric haze, star effects, and a more realistic representation of the Earth. Both modes update in real time - it's just a matter of personal preference!

🔧 Technical
The tracker is built with vanilla HTML, CSS, and JavaScript, and uses Mapbox GL JS for the interactive map. The Easter Bunny's route is pre-planned and stored in a JSON file that the tracker reads at launch. A tick loop running every 250ms interpolates the bunny's position and updates all the HUD statistics in real time.
The tracker uses your IP address with the ipinfo.io service to estimate your general location. This is used solely to calculate the "arrives at your location in about…" estimate. It is not stored, logged, or shared. See our Privacy Policy for more information.
Try the following steps: refresh the page, clear your browser cache, or try a different browser. The tracker works best in up-to-date versions of Chrome, Firefox, Edge, or Safari. If the problem persists, please reach out via the Contact section in the Help menu.
The map requires WebGL support in your browser. Make sure hardware acceleration is enabled in your browser settings. If you're on a very old device or browser, WebGL may not be supported. Try switching from 3D to 2D mode in Settings, which can be less demanding.
Yes! The tracker is designed to be responsive and functional on mobile browsers. For the best experience, we recommend using the tracker on a desktop or laptop with a larger screen, but it works well on phones and tablets too.
The tracker updates every 250 milliseconds - four times per second. The Easter Bunny's position is smoothly interpolated between stops, so his movement on the map appears continuous rather than jumping between locations.
🔒 Privacy & Data
The tracker uses your IP address to estimate your location for the arrival countdown. This lookup is done through ipinfo.io and is not stored by us. Settings are saved locally in your browser and never transmitted anywhere. See the full Privacy Policy for complete details.
All settings are stored in your browser's local storage, entirely on your own device. Nothing is sent to a server. Clearing your browser data will reset your preferences to their defaults.
The tracker itself does not use cookies. Settings are stored via the browser's local storage API rather than cookies. Third-party services used by the tracker (such as Mapbox) may have their own data practices, which are covered by their respective privacy policies.
